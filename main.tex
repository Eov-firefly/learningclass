\section{Abstract}\label{abstract}

An answer-boundary stop can return a locally complete value inside an unfinished artifact, while a grammar-legal continuation can exhaust its token budget before acceptance. We formulate budget-viable commitment as a controller for append-only completion over a declared token language. At activation, selected values are bound and a finite completion language is verified against a consumer contract. The controller retains only tokens with an accepting continuation inside the remaining budget, then commits a validated artifact or records an explicit abstention. We characterize this support by backward reachability and prove conditional completion after entry into a viable state. These properties concern structural acceptance and field preservation, rather than answer truth, and do not establish algorithmic priority over equivalent grammar controllers. A finite-trie implementation integrates with Transformers generation. Controlled CPU checks cover distance pruning, boundary cases, binding, model support and failure accounting. Historical stopping data provide motivation: 204 validity repairs contained 114 correct and 90 incorrect answers, showing that validity and correctness can diverge. A format-only reduction shows that deterministic shortest completion can match acceptance and bound values without additional model calls. In a matched-prefix study of 96 known ARC-Challenge questions per model, the full controller and grammar-only continuation had the same correct-commit counts at a 24-token completion budget: 76 for Qwen and 66 for Mistral. No grammar-legal token was removed in that primary setting. An exploratory tighter budget changed certified coverage, but did not establish a general quality benefit. A subsequent single-GPU study of 64 previously examined questions per model found higher certified coverage for the full controller on initially feasible eight-token requests, with little or no change in correct commitments. Timing for the two-GPU study is descriptive and stratified by two execution waves; the instrumented single-GPU study is not an isolated latency benchmark. The study does not establish equivalence, generalization to unseen tasks, or a GPU speed advantage.

Keywords: constrained decoding; finite languages; token budgets;
verified completion; abstention

\section{1. Introduction}\label{introduction}

Completing an answer field and delivering an accepted artifact are
different events. A numerical answer may be locally complete while a
surrounding wrapper remains open. Conversely, a structurally permitted
continuation may still need more tokens than a request has available.
Returning early, constraining legal prefixes and enforcing a completion
deadline address related but distinct problems.

We study \textbf{budget-viable commitment} over a declared finite token
language. A provisional draft is either accepted directly or used to
bind values and compile verified suffixes. During completion, the
controller excludes next tokens that leave no accepting path within the
remaining budget. A final contract check determines commitment.
Unsupported, infeasible and failed attempts remain observable
abstentions; their work is not omitted from evaluation.

The current implementation is append-only. It can complete a supported
prefix, but cannot undo an earlier answer, edit selected preceding
fields or recover a missed binding event through rollback. The proposal
therefore concerns a specific completion controller, rather than a
general document-repair system. Binding preserves content selected by a
rule; it does not establish that content is true.

The present contributions are a precise separation of acceptance, budget feasibility and answer correctness; a finite-language implementation of the resulting controller with controlled software checks; a retrospective motivation analysis; and a limited real-model known-cohort study with matched baselines and explicitly bounded interpretation. The backward-reachability recurrence itself is not claimed as a novel algorithm. Whether the controller has an independent methodological advantage over existing structured generation remains unestablished.

Three questions organize the study. RQ1 asks whether the implemented controller satisfies its conditional structural and binding invariants. RQ2 asks whether budget pruning improves completion and correct-commit yield over matched grammar-only continuation. RQ3 asks whether model-scored completion provides value beyond deterministic repair at an acceptable total cost. Controlled checks address RQ1 on finite examples; the matched-prefix and wrapper studies in Section 6 provide limited evidence for RQ2 and RQ3, while the remaining end-to-end and independent evaluations are still needed. Historical stopping results in Section 5 do not evaluate the new controller.

\section{2. Related work and comparison
boundary}\label{related-work-and-comparison-boundary}

Lexically constrained decoding and grammar-constrained generation are
established approaches (Hokamp and Liu, 2017; Geng et al., 2023).
Practical libraries such as XGrammar and Outlines support structured
generation (XGrammar project, 2026; Outlines project, 2026), and native
Transformers stopping criteria support token-boundary return decisions
(Hugging Face, 2025). These capabilities are relevant baselines, rather
than evidence that the present controller is distinct.

Budget-aware reasoning addresses how much inference to allocate (Wang et
al., 2024; Han et al., 2025; Muennighoff et al., 2025). Our stated
budget is the number of additional output tokens before acceptance in a
declared controller language. This differs from estimating reasoning
sufficiency: the controller can ensure a permitted structural ending
without ensuring that the answer is correct.

An implementation with the same token transitions, acceptance set, distance filter and scoring rule can be algorithmically equivalent to ours. Renaming that implementation or adding a submission certificate does not establish a new decoding paradigm. The verified XGrammar documentation also identifies its XGrammar-2 successor as dynamic structured generation; comparison cannot be restricted to old static-grammar examples. The documentation was checked, and Section 6 includes an XGrammar baseline for the declared finite-language study; that experiment is not a comparison with every capability of XGrammar-2, nor a completed full-paper survey.

The present literature boundary is consequently limited. The scope must
be checked against deadline-aware constrained search, structured editing
and verified generation before asserting priority. No claim is made that
these fields lack the same recurrence. A useful systems or task
contribution would require measured value beyond equivalent grammar
control and deterministic completion.

\section{3. Formal controller}\label{formal-controller}

\subsection{3.1. Consumer contract, provisional prefix and
binding}\label{consumer-contract-provisional-prefix-and-binding}

Let \(x\) be the prompt and \(y\) the generated output prefix. A
declared contract \(\mathcal C\) has a final acceptance predicate
\(V_{\mathcal C}(y)\) and a task extractor \(g\). A binding rule
identifies values \(b\) to preserve. Define \(P_b(y)\) as their
preservation predicate. Neither \(V_{\mathcal C}\) nor \(P_b\) is a
gold-answer oracle. Correctness, when a reference exists, is evaluated
separately through \(g\).

At an activation prefix, compilation constructs a token controller with
partial transition \(\delta(s,v)\) and accepting states \(F\). An
accepting path must decode to an artifact satisfying both
\(V_{\mathcal C}\) and \(P_b\). This is a soundness requirement for the
declared predicates, not a claim to implement all Markdown, LaTeX or
JSON semantics. The current wrapper validator is a limited operational
heuristic. An independent final call to the predicate rechecks each
committed artifact; it is not an independently developed semantic
oracle.

The protocol has four persistent states: DRAFT, REPAIR, COMMIT and
ABSTAIN. BIND is a transition operation that fixes values and constructs
the completion controller, rather than a separately persisted state in
the supplied code. Already accepted prefixes commit directly. The
current interfaces append tokens to the entire frozen prefix; they do
not implement selective rewriting of earlier unbound text.

\subsection{3.2. Budget and token
language}\label{budget-and-token-language}

Let \(B\) be the total allowed number of generated output tokens and let
\(t\) have already been generated. The repair allowance is \(r=B-t\). In
a supplied-prefix experiment, the allowance instead refers only to new
completion tokens, and the supplied prefix's cost must be reported
separately. Prompt tokens do not count toward this output cap, but their
processing time remains part of a declared request measurement.

Each transition consumes one model token. Acceptance triggers an
immediate return action without requiring an additional EOS token. EOS
is forbidden inside a nonaccepting repair. Any emitted EOS during
drafting still counts as a generated token. If a different
implementation requires EOS to complete an artifact, that transition and
its cost must be included in its controller.

For state \(s\), define the distance within the declared controller
language:

\[d(s)=\min\{|z|:\delta^*(s,z)\in F\},\]

with \(d(s)=\infty\) for unreachable acceptance and \(d(s)=0\) at an
accepting state. For \(s\notin F\) and \(r>0\), define

\[\mathcal A_r(s)=\{v:\delta(s,v)\text{ is defined},\ d(\delta(s,v))\le r-1\}.\]

The distance is measured in model tokens, not characters or bytes.
Exactness is relative to this controller: a finite suffix list with
selected tokenizations can omit other valid strings and other
tokenizations of the same string. Its distance is not a global minimum
over all artifacts accepted by the consumer.

\subsection{3.3. Model scores and viable
support}\label{model-scores-and-viable-support}

For positive normalizer \(Z\), the locally masked distribution is

\[q_r(v\mid x,y,s)=\frac{p_\theta(v\mid x,y)\mathbf 1\{v\in\mathcal A_r(s)\}}{Z},\qquad Z=\sum_u p_\theta(u\mid x,y)\mathbf 1\{u\in\mathcal A_r(s)\}.\]

The implementation uses greedy selection among permitted finite scores.
Negative infinity may represent zero model support; it is harmless if
another permitted token has a finite score. An all-negative-infinity
support, NaN or positive infinity on permitted entries produces an
explicit failure. No assertion is made that local masking samples from
the model conditioned on eventual global acceptance.

\subsection{3.4. Reachability characterization and conditional
completion}\label{reachability-characterization-and-conditional-completion}

\textbf{Proposition 1 --- feasible first-token characterization.} For a
nonaccepting state and remaining budget \(r>0\), a legal token \(v\)
belongs to \(\mathcal A_r(s)\) if and only if some accepting
continuation of at most \(r\) tokens begins with \(v\).

\textbf{Proof.} If \(v\) is retained, the next state has an accepting
suffix of length at most \(r-1\), which can follow \(v\). Conversely,
remove the first token from any accepting path of length at most \(r\);
its suffix witnesses \(d(\delta(s,v))\le r-1\).

Thus \(\mathcal A_r\) is maximally permissive with respect to
existential structural feasibility in this language. This
characterization is ordinary backward reachability; it is not a priority
claim. Structural feasibility does not ensure that the retained path has
positive model probability or a correct answer.

\textbf{Proposition 2 --- nonempty support and budget-bounded
acceptance.} Suppose repair begins at \(s_0\) with \(d(s_0)\le r_0\).
Assume exact transitions and distances, a permitted token with positive
model support at every nonaccepting step, selection restricted to the
viable support, and successful execution without an external failure.
Then structural acceptance occurs in at most \(r_0\) new tokens.

\textbf{Proof.} A shortest path from any nonaccepting state with
\(d(s)\le r\) supplies an allowed first token, so the structural support
is nonempty. After any allowed emission, decrementing \(r\) preserves
\(d(s)\le r\). At zero remaining tokens, this inequality implies
\(d(s)=0\), hence acceptance. The model-support and execution
assumptions ensure the process does not abort earlier. Immediate return
at acceptance ends the process without another token.

\textbf{Proposition 3 --- certified output.} If all reachable accepting
states decode to artifacts satisfying \(V_{\mathcal C}\) and \(P_b\),
and commitment revalidates those predicates, every committed artifact
satisfies them. This is a contract-relative postcondition. It provides
no factual or task-correctness guarantee and cannot establish soundness
of the validator itself.

The statements are conditional on reaching a viable activation point.
They do not guarantee a candidate, supported compilation or successful
commitment for every request. An optimistic lower bound on \(d\) is
insufficient: it can retain a path that misses the deadline. These
results also concern a token budget, not a bound on wall-clock latency.

\subsection{3.5. Equivalence, deterministic reduction and a quality
counterexample}\label{equivalence-deterministic-reduction-and-a-quality-counterexample}

Under the same state, language, distance oracle, scores, remaining
budget, acceptance rule and tie-breaking, another grammar engine
applying \(\mathcal A_r\) has the same token support and greedy
trajectory. If every grammar-legal next token already satisfies the
bound, the distance mask is inactive. Such matched cases are consistency
controls rather than opportunities for an output-quality advantage.

\textbf{Proposition 4 --- format-only reduction.} Suppose a viable
compiled repair language contains only formatting variants of the same
bound complete answer. A deterministic shortest accepting path preserves
that answer and satisfies the same contract without a new model call.
Its inserted suffix uses no more tokens than any other accepting path in
that language.

\textbf{Proof.} Select a shortest accepting path of length \(d(s)\). The
compiler's postconditions establish acceptance and preservation.
Selecting and serializing a path requires no probability-model query.

This reduction applies directly to the wrapper prototype. Deterministic
insertions still have CPU and serialization costs, and the common prefix
retains its model-generation cost. The result does not compare total
latency without measurement, nor does it apply when choosing an unbound
field changes task correctness.

A two-branch counterexample shows why structural feasibility cannot
replace quality evaluation. Let one accepting path contain token \(a\)
and encode a wrong answer, while a correct answer requires tokens
\(b,c\). With one remaining token, the mask can retain the wrong
one-token path and exclude the correct two-token path. Commitment
remains structurally valid. Binding can likewise exclude a correct
answer before repair begins. Therefore neither correct-commit
improvement nor noninferiority follows from Propositions 1--3 or from
historical zero-loss observations.

\section{4. Reference implementation and
verification}\label{reference-implementation-and-verification}

\subsection{4.1. Operational activation
rule}\label{operational-activation-rule}

The draft-to-wrapper interface uses a total cap \(B\) and a fixed
reserve \(h\), with \(0\le h<B\). After each draft token, it checks the
following rules in order:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  If the current artifact satisfies the closure-balanced reference
  predicate, commit it.
\item
  If natural EOS has occurred without acceptance, abstain.
\item
  If the strict marker predicate identifies a complete candidate, bind
  that value and propose supported closing suffixes. Verify their
  decoded context, final acceptance and candidate preservation. Enter
  repair only when a compiled accepting suffix fits \(B-t\); otherwise
  abstain.
\item
  If no candidate was found and \(t\ge B-h\), abstain; otherwise
  continue drafting.
\end{enumerate}

Candidate recognition takes precedence over the no-candidate deadline at
the same step, while the total cap still applies. The reserve is a
drafting policy, not an oracle guaranteeing a candidate by the deadline.
Unsupported candidate states currently abstain immediately rather than
continuing to search for a later candidate. This behavior must be held
fixed or explicitly ablated in experiments.

The supplied-prefix interface starts directly from a finite language and
an acceptance/binding predicate pair. An already accepted prefix commits
with zero new tokens and zero model calls, including at zero remaining
budget. Other prefixes require a viable nonempty completion. The caller
supplies the prefix and suffix language without a gold-answer argument.
This API separation alone cannot certify that a caller's predicates are
gold-independent; benchmark construction must be audited separately.

\subsection{4.2. Compilation, complexity and
scope}\label{compilation-complexity-and-scope}

Each proposed suffix is tokenized and decoded after the actual output
prefix; paths failing exact context round-trip, final acceptance or
preservation are rejected. The verified token paths form a trie. If
their total length is \(M\), the trie has at most \(M+1\) nodes.
Building it is \(O(M)\); a postorder distance calculation uses
\(O(N+E)\) time and memory for \(N\) nodes and \(E\) edges. These bounds
exclude tokenization, candidate enumeration and predicate execution.
Enumeration may be impractical for a large compositional answer space.

The current implementation traverses the emitted repair prefix when
retrieving a trie state and constructs a dense vocabulary mask. At
repair step \(j\), this adds prefix traversal and outgoing-edge
inspection plus \(O(|\mathcal V|)\) mask construction, before accounting
for the model, decoding or validation. Python callbacks and score checks
may synchronize host and device. No near-zero overhead claim follows
from the linear distance preprocessing bound.

The implementation is batch-size-one greedy generation with no KV
rollback, speculative path, arbitrary JSON Schema compiler or general
document grammar. Two interfaces demonstrate wrapper closure and finite
structured completion, respectively. The latter allows selection of an
unbound field from supplied alternatives, but is not itself a dataset
benchmark or a general grammar compiler.

\subsection{4.3. Controlled software
checks}\label{controlled-software-checks}

The revised code passed 22 controlled CPU tests using PyTorch 2.6 and
Transformers 4.48.2 with a small random GPT2 and scripted scores. Checks
include finite-trie distance and pruning invariants; candidate and
identifier preservation; choice of an unbound label; unsupported syntax
and context mismatch; invalid EOS and drafting deadlines; deterministic
repair; accepting and nonaccepting zero-budget cases; zero model support
and invalid scores; and retention of trace and cost after
final-certificate or runtime failure.

The new edge-case checks repair accounting and integration behavior
rather than measuring answer quality. Both generation adapters return
explicit constraint/runtime failure records when handled, retain
available generated tokens and remove their model hooks. Forward counts
record entered calls, including calls that raise an error; they do not
count only successful returns. A benchmark wrapper must still capture
configuration, tokenization, predicate, device and infrastructure
exceptions outside these handled paths. These tests establish no
pretrained-model accuracy, practical compilation coverage or CUDA
speedup.

\section{5. Historical motivation and evidence
limits}\label{historical-motivation-and-evidence-limits}

The historical stopping experiments used Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-1M (Yang et
al., 2025) and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 (Mistral AI, 2024) on GSM8K
(Cobbe et al., 2021) and ARC-Challenge (Clark et al., 2018). There were
600 main questions per task, reused across models. Uniform ran to EOS or
a 512-token cap. Complete returned the earliest certified terminal
answer line. The full initial 25,200-call protocol and all ten
historical table bodies remain in Supplement A--B; the later timing
study added 11,200 scored calls and 480 warmups. Repeated calls and
sessions are not independent new questions.

The frozen quality contract required a last line-start \texttt{\#\#\#\#}
value with a newline or EOS certificate and no later non-whitespace
content. It did not validate the surrounding document. It differs from
both the prototype's closure-balanced heuristic and a future
typed-record contract. All paired scores in Table 1 use the frozen
historical contract.

\section{6. New empirical evaluation and remaining protocol}

\subsection{6.1. Study design and data integrity}

We conducted a limited known-cohort evaluation using Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-1M and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 on two A800 80 GB GPUs. The matched-prefix task used 96 ARC-Challenge evaluation questions and 16 development questions from an already inspected cohort; these are not an independent unseen test set. The answer-label and option-text representations are two encodings of the same questions, not separate tasks. The supplied prefix and finite candidate language were held fixed within matched comparisons. The completion budget counts newly generated suffix tokens and excludes the supplied prefix. The measured request includes per-request language construction, prompt preparation and, when inference occurs, processing the supplied prompt and prefix; it does not include generating the supplied prefix upstream. The comparison covers the declared finite language and wrapper contract, not arbitrary grammars or full document semantics.

The frozen study contains 6,976 records across all tracks and repetitions, including 1,248 deterministic CPU records; this is not a count of independent questions or GPU inference calls. Offline checks found the expected complete grid with no duplicate or missing records, inference errors, invalid constrained commits, budget overruns or format-reduction mismatches. These are technical integrity checks, not independent human semantic annotation. The format-only reduction and a limited wrapper end-to-end track were also evaluated. On the wrapper track, the full controller certified 27 of 32 Qwen and 30 of 32 Mistral requests; no legal token was removed in that track. These observations do not isolate a binding or budget-pruning effect.

\subsection{6.2. Primary matched-prefix results}

The designated primary family compared the full distance-filtered controller with grammar-only continuation using option-text encoding and a 24-token completion budget. Each model was assessed on the same 96 questions under both policies. The endpoint is correct certified commitments over all eligible questions; a certified output is not necessarily correct. The XGrammar and candidate-sequence-scoring baselines are retained as additional controls, not as interchangeable implementations of the primary comparison. The XGrammar finite-string language can permit tokenization paths that differ from the canonical token trie, so differences from that baseline cannot be attributed to the distance filter alone.

\subsection{6.3. Exploratory tight-budget and format results}

At the 8-token option-text budget, certified coverage under full versus grammar-only was 84/96 versus 69/96 for Qwen and 65/96 versus 53/96 for Mistral. Correct certified submissions were 54/96 for both Qwen policies and 41/96 versus 39/96 for Mistral. In this setting the full filter removed grammar-legal tokens, with 50 and 42 removals respectively. These observations are exploratory: certification and correctness are different endpoints, and the small known cohort and multiple settings do not establish a general or statistically significant quality improvement. Infeasible requests under the full controller were recorded as abstentions, not invalid submissions.

In the finite format-only controls, deterministic shortest completion and the full controller matched the reported acceptance and bound-value checks when completion was feasible; both abstained below the compiled minimum. This is consistent with the reduction in Section 3.5, but does not extend the deterministic result to task-relevant unbound answers.

\subsection{6.4. Single-GPU exploratory controllability study}

A subsequent frozen control study ran Qwen and Mistral sequentially on one A800 80 GB GPU. Its 64 ARC-Challenge questions were drawn from the already inspected cohort; the study is exploratory and does not supply an independent test set. Across two models, 64 questions, three budget conditions and five policies, it produced 1,920 records. Offline integrity checks found a complete grid, zero inference errors, invalid submissions or budget overruns, and no lost certified or correct commitments in the full-versus-grammar comparisons. The two model runs completed with zero exit codes. These are computational checks, not human semantic annotation.

The prespecified within-study comparison considered the eight-token cap only among requests initially feasible for the shared canonical token language. The full distance-filtered controller and grammar-only continuation shared this language and the same matched inputs. Table 3 also reports correct certified commitments over all 64 eligible requests: certified completion is a structural endpoint and does not imply a correct answer. The seven Qwen and 19 Mistral requests initially infeasible at this cap are excluded from the feasible-subset rate, but remain in the all-request correctness denominator.

Incorrect certified submissions also increased from nine to 22 for Qwen and from 10 to 17 for Mistral, emphasizing that structural completion is not answer quality.

Two case-dependent boundary controls help interpret the mechanism. At each case's compiled minimum canonical suffix length, full versus grammar-only certified 64/64 versus 24/64 for Qwen and 64/64 versus 26/64 for Mistral. At a wider cap defined by each case's maximum canonical suffix length, both policies certified 64/64 for both models. These deliberately constructed caps are not a deployment-budget distribution, and the three conditions reuse the same questions. At the fixed eight-token cap, candidate-sequence scoring certified 57 and 45 requests and yielded 34 and 27 correct submissions for Qwen and Mistral, respectively; XGrammar certified 39 and 37 and yielded 31 and 27 correct submissions. XGrammar may allow tokenizations outside the canonical trie, so its difference cannot be attributed to distance pruning alone. Deterministic shortest completion also certified all initially feasible fixed-eight requests, with nine correct Qwen and 11 correct Mistral submissions; its completion guarantee is therefore another relevant structural comparator. The 1,920 records include 384 deterministic cases without model inference. The instrumentation serves correctness analysis and does not establish isolated latency or speed.

\subsection{6.6. Remaining tracks and task construction}

The matched-prefix track above holds the prefix, token language and remaining budget fixed. A broader end-to-end track should include prompt processing, drafting, failed activation, compilation, completion, validation and abstention; the limited wrapper run above does not replace that evaluation. Supplied-prefix timing cannot support end-to-end speed or coverage claims.

The task set must distinguish a format-only reduction control from structured prediction with task-relevant unbound fields. The former compares deterministic closure with model-scored closure without presuming a semantic benefit. The latter can bind a public instance identifier while leaving a categorical or numeric answer to predict. ARC candidates may include all visible options. A numeric answer language must be defined independently of gold answers; a manually supplied reference-answer list is invalid. The present wrapper interface cannot execute a general numeric-grammar task without additional compiler and runner development.

Historical cohorts have been inspected and can support a declared known-cohort re-evaluation. They cannot be relabeled unseen holdouts. Independent instances or tasks are needed for broader generalization claims. Input IDs, contracts, compiler capabilities and binding rules will be frozen before final evaluation. Failed compilation remains in the denominator for every frozen eligible request.

\subsection{6.7. Further comparators and ablations}

Further matched-prefix comparisons should include deterministic shortest completion, model-scored grammar-only continuation, the full budget mask and an independently implemented version of the same distance recurrence. Shared language and acceptance checks are essential. The equivalent recurrence checks consistency; output superiority is not expected under identical assumptions. Finite-answer tasks also require a strong simple alternative, such as development-frozen prior selection or candidate-sequence scoring with all model calls accounted for.

End-to-end comparisons require Uniform, early return on the same target contract, deterministic repair, selective grammar-only repair, the full controller and a practical grammar engine operating from the start. Native string stopping is sufficient competition only when it enforces the declared contract. From-start and selective grammar differ in more than the budget filter and must be interpreted as system comparisons. Historical Complete under its old contract remains a diagnostic, not a primary new-contract comparator.

Ablations isolate removal of the distance filter at the same binding point, selective versus from-start constraints, and complete-answer versus public-structure binding. Any comparison requiring earlier-field editing first requires a new implementation. A deliberately removed validator is a fault-injection check rather than a competitive baseline.

\subsection{6.8. Further budgets, endpoints and failure reporting}

Development data will determine tight, intermediate and relaxed total token caps and the reserve rule before testing. Shared-prefix cases include budgets below, at and above the compiled minimum distance; unequal-length accepting branches; inactive masks; and infeasible states. Report how often pruning actually excludes a grammar-legal token. A fixed single cap cannot establish a budget-response curve.

For request $i$, let $D_i$ indicate a declared submission rather than abstention, $A_i=D_i\mathbf 1\{V_{\mathcal C}(y_i)\land P_b(y_i)\}$ indicate a certified submission, and $Q_i=A_i\mathbf 1\{g(y_i)=g_i^*\}$ indicate a correct certified submission. The primary endpoint is $\sum_i Q_i/n$ over all frozen eligible requests. Certified coverage is $\sum_i A_i/n$, and conditional accuracy is $\sum_i Q_i/\sum_i A_i$, undefined when no output is certified. Invalid submissions, $\sum_i D_i(1-A_i)$, and binding violations remain separately visible. For a baseline without controller states, a normal returned output is a declared submission, then scored by the same predicates. Invalid raw returns are retained rather than silently relabeled or discarded. A schema check that rejects outputs can change declared submission counts without improving the all-request quality endpoint.

Also report budget overruns and failure categories: unsupported compilation, context mismatch, no candidate, infeasible distance, invalid EOS, constraint failures and runtime errors. Infrastructure failures need a prespecified retry rule and their own labels; no failed request may silently disappear. No ratio with a zero denominator is assigned an invented zero or perfect value.

Length-dependent pruning may favor short wrong answers. Include canonical short-label controls, answer-length strata, erroneous bindings and offline reference-reachability analysis. Reference information is available only to the scorer, never to inference, budget selection or post hoc sample exclusion. Supported-language or reference-reachable subsets are diagnostics rather than replacements for the all-request endpoint.

\subsection{6.9. Further cost, statistics and reproducibility}

Record model-generated, deterministically inserted and returned token IDs separately, together with forward calls and costs of compilation, masking, validation and failed attempts. Measure synchronized isolated request latency with matched model precision, attention backend, batch size and hardware; randomize or balance policy order. Separate cold compilation from a declared reusable-cache regime. Report mean and P50/P95 latency and paired uncertainty. Concurrent throughput would require a distinct experiment, not extrapolation from request times.

A pilot will support sample-size planning from a prespecified meaningful correct-commit difference and paired discordance; timing planning uses paired variability. Repeated calls, budgets and model answers to the same question are not independent questions. Question-level pairing and clustering must preserve these dependencies. A primary setting or multiplicity adjustment must be fixed before confirmatory cross-budget and cross-model claims. All cells and unfavorable results remain reportable.

The archive will contain frozen manifests, version and environment hashes, full token traces, binding and distance events, statuses/errors, request-time boundaries and executable scoring/analysis. An audited separation of controller inputs from reference labels is required. The primary matched-prefix comparison reported above is distinct from the additional, as-yet-unexecuted evaluation described in this section; its known-cohort observations must not be presented as independent confirmation.

\section{7. Discussion}

The controller adds a remaining-budget condition to structural prefix legality and makes acceptance a distinct return event. Its conditional guarantee is useful for specifying behavior, but follows from reachability over a declared language. It cannot alone support a paradigm claim relative to an equivalent grammar implementation. A larger experimental advantage must be attributed to a documented difference in language, activation, scoring, compiler cost or task behavior, rather than a name.

The current finite language limits both coverage and scalability. Missing valid paths can cause unnecessary abstention, and an exhaustive language over rich fields can be expensive to construct. The wrapper contract is a heuristic rather than a full document parser. Append-only binding may freeze an incorrect answer and cannot use later correction. Validity is consequently a necessary interface property, not a proxy for reasoning quality.

The deterministic reduction is a substantive boundary. If empirical gains are restricted to formatting-equivalent suffixes, the appropriate contribution is verified interface repair. Task-relevant unbound fields may justify model-scored continuation, but they also expose quality loss through budget pruning and require strong non-generative or candidate-scoring baselines. The known-cohort comparison against grammar-only and candidate scoring is informative but limited, particularly because the primary budget filter was inactive.

The two-GPU study found no correct-commit difference in its primary matched-prefix cells. The subsequent single-GPU controls show that the budget filter can preserve structural completion when grammar-only paths run out of budget on this known cohort, while the all-request correct counts changed little or not at all. Both observations at the tighter budget are exploratory. It establishes neither a general task-quality improvement nor a latency advantage. The historical studies support their own stopping and monitoring conclusions; the CPU tests support implementation behavior on controlled finite cases. Independent data, broader task runs and source-level comparison with relevant prior systems are required before a stronger contribution claim can be assessed.

\section{8. Conclusions}

Budget-viable commitment provides an explicit finite-language specification for append-only constrained completion and validated return. Exact token-level reachability gives conditional structural completion inside a remaining budget, while final acceptance checks preserve declared contract and binding predicates. These properties do not imply true answers, global compiler completeness, successful activation for every request or wall-clock speed. The finite-trie implementation and controlled checks make the specification testable. Historical evidence motivates separating validity from correctness, and the format-only reduction identifies a necessary deterministic comparator. The two-GPU known-cohort study finds no primary correct-commit difference when the filter is inactive. The later single-GPU controls expose a structural completion difference at a tighter cap on reused questions, without establishing a general accuracy benefit. Independent task and full-system cost comparisons remain necessary before a broader empirical algorithm claim.
